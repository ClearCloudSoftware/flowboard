The queue you work, not a list you browse

Agents run unattended until they need a human. Everything that needs one lands here. The screen is built around a single claim: you should be able to empty it without touching the mouse — so one card is open at a time, the number keys always mean the thing in front of you, and answering closes the card and opens the next.

The five question shapes

Agents can only ask in these five shapes, and the same shape looks and behaves the same everywhere you might answer it — here, or in chat. That constraint is what makes a keyboard contract possible at all: the number keys can always mean something.

Free text is the escape hatch, not the default. An agent that asks everything as free text has given up on being answerable in three seconds. If you keep seeing it, the skill posing the questions is the thing to fix.

The four kinds of card

Why five questions are one card

When a step needs several things decided, it asks for all of them at once and parks. That is deliberate, and the reason is on the card: the run is waiting for the whole set. Answering one at a time would mean the agent wakes, finds it still can't proceed, and parks again — restarting your wait each time and paying to re-read its own context.

Bigger chunks are faster and use more memory on the worker.

"5 of 5 answered" can include a blank. Free text counts as answered even when empty, because "nothing to add" is a real answer. Every other shape has to be given a value before Submit lights up.

What a waiting question costs, and who can answer it

The single most useful fact about this screen: a parked run is not billing you. When an agent asks, it saves its place and lets go of its worker. Leaving the inbox full costs calendar time and a worker slot's worth of throughput — not money.

The escalation timer

Ten minutes without an answer and the question is posted to your chat tool with the same controls on it. The card shows the countdown first, then the state:

Whoever answers first wins

Answer here and the chat message updates to show it's handled. Answer in chat and the card closes here. The same question is never open in two places once it's been answered in one.

An answer from someone else does not move your cursor. If a teammate closes a card while you are mid-batch, that card disappears and you carry on where you were — the queue re-orders around you rather than under you.

Every control

Keyboard

This is the screen with the strongest keyboard claim in the product, and the claim rests on one rule: there is only ever one open card, so a key never has to ask "which one?".

Number keys toggle in a pick-many. In every other shape they select and replace. It is the one place a key does something different, and the checkbox in the control is what tells you which you're in.
